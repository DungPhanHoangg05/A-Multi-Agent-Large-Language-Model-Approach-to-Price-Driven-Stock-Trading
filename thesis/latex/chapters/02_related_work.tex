\chapter{Related Work}\label{s:9827c64bef49c4b1}
\section{Signals, selection and financial evaluation}\label{s:14b05e24677e26cb}
Technical analysis converts observable price and volume patterns into
testable hypotheses. Lo, Mamaysky and Wang~\cite{lo2000technical} formalize chart-pattern
recognition and examine conditional return distributions, providing a
way to study patterns beyond subjective inspection. Their evidence does
not validate every indicator or establish profitability after transaction
costs. Accordingly, the present system separates
features, reports, directional proposals and executed actions.

Kakushadze's formulaic-alpha library~\cite{kakushadze2016formulaic} supplies explicit transformations.
Cross-sectional rank and group neutralization operate across securities;
temporal operators operate over a security's history. Replacing the
former with temporal ranks in a single-stock implementation changes the
formula's meaning. A typical-price proxy also differs from authenticated
intraday volume-weighted average price (VWAP). The thesis attributes
these foundations while treating its adapted library and five additional
factors as a distinct implementation family. Our system and Common further
differ in normalization, orientation, selection and report projection.

Ordinary quantitative controls help locate the behavior added by an agent
system. Constant actions expose class imbalance and capital exposure;
momentum and logistic rules provide simpler predictors. Scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}
supports several such controls, but a library citation does not specify
their fitting recipe. Selection and
assessment must also remain separate. Varma and Simon~\cite{varma2006bias} demonstrate bias
when an optimized validation score is reused as an independent error
estimate; their classification experiments do not by themselves validate
financial time-series splitting. The numerical
factor controls in this thesis evaluate their own selection rules, not
the isolated contribution of an LLM factor selector.

\section{Specialist financial agents and input modalities}\label{s:f7594e9d067d74c3}
QuantAgent~\cite{xiong2025quantagent,yresearch2026quantagentcode} is the direct architectural predecessor: Indicator, Pattern,
Trend and Risk roles supply complementary price-derived analyses, with
a separately described final DecisionAgent. Its versioned paper studies
one-hour/four-hour direction benchmarks and risk bounds. The thesis
adapts state contracts, chart tools, orchestration and interface elements,
then uses daily LONG/CASH evaluation with its own fixed net-cycle oracle.
The inspected
upstream comparison commit is recorded in the third-party notices; the
originally adopted commit was not recorded. The adaptation is not a claim
to have invented specialist financial agents or reproduced upstream
performance.

TradingAgents~\cite{tradingagents2024} organizes analysts, research debate, trading and risk teams
using fundamental, sentiment, news and technical inputs. Its historical
US-stock simulation and buy/sell/hold accounting differ from the present
contract. FinVision~\cite{fatemi2024finvision} combines news summaries,
visual technical analysis and textual/visual reflection before a trading
recommendation. Its use of previous signals and outcomes is related to
historical context, but reflection does not itself define a Bayesian
posterior or a numerical multi-task model.

Chart interpretation and news classification have separate validation
problems. ChartLlama~\cite{han2023chartllama} studies chart instruction and understanding tasks;
its results do not certify another model's candlestick reasoning, and its
weights are not used here. ViSoBERT~\cite{nguyen2023visobert} supplies
a Vietnamese social-media backbone, while the pinned 5CD resource~\cite{hf5cdsentiment} supplies
the downstream three-label sentiment classifier. Their domains and roles
are distinct. Neither source establishes historical financial-news
availability, market-domain accuracy or probability calibration in this
study.

\section{Regimes and historical-context inference}\label{s:72ac96e2a9d3b286}
Hamilton's regime-switching account~\cite{hamilton2005regimes} models persistent changes through a
latent state; the state is an estimated representation rather than an
observed economic truth. Rabiner~\cite{rabiner1989hmm} separates hidden Markov model (HMM) transition and emission models, parameter fitting and state inference.
Forward/backward inference makes the information boundary explicit:
smoothing an earlier state can use later observations from the supplied
sequence. Both the fitting period and inference prefix therefore matter.
This thesis uses distinct frozen signal-bank and
numerical regime resources; they can assign different states to the same
date. A matched comparison is still needed to isolate regime value.

In-context examples can change a model's predictions without changing its
weights. Xie et al.~\cite{xie2022implicitbayes} analyze an implicit Bayesian interpretation under a
specified mixture-of-HMMs pretraining construction; the assumptions do
not prove that any hosted trading LLM computes a financial posterior.
Rubin et al.~\cite{rubin2022retrieve} learn example retrieval for
semantic parsing. Retrieval is an information-selection mechanism, not
automatically a Bayesian update.

These distinctions apply directly here. BICL v1 reports empirical
population rates and selected episodes; its name does not make the rates
Beta-posterior means. C2 instead uses explicit count-based shrinkage and
readiness gates. The alternative named B\_semantic presents counts and
intervals; it is not an embedding retriever. Counts, top-$K$ examples and
numerical task datasets have different denominators and functions.

\section{Multi-task Bayesian ICL and numerical students}\label{s:b46bc1f71efb86f5}
Prior-Data Fitted Networks (PFNs)~\cite{muller2022transformers} train on datasets sampled from a specified
prior, approximating a posterior-predictive mapping through prior-data
negative log-likelihood. A sampled training prior is central to this
construction; it is not generic prompting of a pretrained language model.
Zhu, Oermann and Cho~\cite{zhu2026multitask} extend context to
multiple prior datasets, target observations and an unlabeled target
query while retaining task boundaries. Their hierarchical predictive
adaptation is studied in synthetic regression and temperature prediction,
not Vietnamese stock trading.

The thesis adapts the task distinction to historical same-stock blocks.
A fitted mixture of sixteen Beta components supplies count-based teacher
probabilities. Synthetic distillation trains a small numerical student
on soft query targets; sampled features are independent of synthetic
latent rates and labels. The local two-block/four-head architecture and
objective differ from the reference paper's eight-layer/eight-head
synthetic setup and target-observation likelihood training. Names
containing ``replica'' identify local resources, not exact replication.

At market inference the numerical weights are frozen. Context can change,
and a hosted Decision LLM receives a bounded text projection. A magnitude
model and an external utility gate decide whether a proposal may replace
Fact Anchor. Teacher approximation, market probability calibration, LLM
verbal confidence and final action are separate objects. Synthetic
agreement does not establish the student's causal contribution to returns.

\section{Dependent comparisons and reproducibility}\label{s:eabd76a8c5a70fc6}
Arnott, Harvey and Markowitz~\cite{arnott2019backtesting} emphasize a financial research protocol that
addresses data, selection, shortfall and changing markets; prediction
scores require explicit execution assumptions.
White~\cite{white2000reality} explains how specification search and repeated data use can produce
apparently favorable results. A chronological
split does not remove prior historical exposure, overlapping label
windows or feature-construction leakage. Shared upstream reports make
method comparisons conditional on the same realized information, whereas
repeating only Decision measures conditional response variation.

Uncertainty methods retain assumptions. Politis and Romano's stationary
bootstrap~\cite{politis1994stationary} motivates preserving dependence, but differs from the recorded
fixed circular blocks and whole-fold resampling here.
Newey--West covariance estimation~\cite{newey1987hac} addresses
heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation under regularity conditions; a stored heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent (HAC) diagnostic is not a small-sample guarantee.
Holm adjustment~\cite{holm1979multiple} requires valid underlying tests
and cannot repair unverified sign-symmetry or post-inference selection.
Six already exposed folds support descriptive
and sensitivity analysis, not confirmed significance or calibrated band
coverage. The thesis reports its actual estimators without claiming to
have run these papers' procedures where it did not.

Stored sources, prompts, responses and checkpoints support inspection and
offline replay. A new hosted request can still change with service/model
versions; input authenticity and prospective evaluation require additional
evidence. A source hash alone cannot supply either.
