\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) provide a flexible interface for combining numerical signals, news and chart descriptions in financial decision support. Multi-agent systems such as QuantAgent \cite{xiong2025quantagent} assign these inputs to specialised analytical roles, but their predictive and economic value depends on how the evidence is constructed, combined and evaluated. Vietnamese equities offer a useful setting because local-language news, single-stock data and cash-equity settlement require explicit adaptations.

We integrate an OHLCV alpha selector and Vietnamese sentiment with Indicator, Pattern, Trend and Decision roles adapted from QuantAgent. The selector ranks 85 candidate factors, while sentiment enters a separate textual report. Numerical indicators, chart descriptions and the enabled factor/news reports are condensed into the Decision prompt with heuristic evidence priorities. The resulting binary forecast is evaluated against a net three-session LONG cycle; a bearish forecast maps to CASH in the long-only account.

The empirical analysis comprises two studies on 102 eligible stock--date observations across nine equities, twelve dates and six historical blocks. The component study compares Full, which includes factors and sentiment, with Base, which omits both, alongside class-aware and non-LLM controls. Small repeated panels examine upstream variability, factor-only and sentiment-only variants, and a matched-structure historical placebo. The historical-context study evaluates Bayesian and conditional priors, a fact anchor and multi-task Bayesian in-context learning (MT-BICL), using three Decision repeats conditional on one shared upstream realization. Each study uses its own model responses and statistical protocol.

In the component study, Full balanced accuracy (BA) is \FullBA\%, compared with \BaseBA\% for Base and \LogisticPriceBA\% for a price-only logistic comparator. Full and Base both capture seven of the 25 UP outcomes, with Full producing fewer false LONG forecasts. Their mean separate-account net returns are \FullReturn\% and \BaseReturn\%. In the historical-context study, the Bayesian prior improves mean return over the Common baseline by $+\UnifiedVOneCommonReturnEffect$ percentage points, but differs from Native by $\UnifiedVOneNativeReturnEffect$ points. All research methods have negative mean account returns.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A Vietnamese-equity implementation that combines an 85-candidate OHLCV selector and local-language sentiment with shared chart-agent reports.
\item A specified distinction between native adaptive factor diagnostics and a training-fitted causal projection used by historical-context methods.
\item A paired evaluation of component variants, numerical baselines and historical-context policies, with explicit missing-input, support and fallback rules.
\item An analysis connecting class-aware forecasts, repeated hosted decisions and transaction-cost-aware account outcomes.
\end{itemize}

The analysis is historical and exploratory: the evaluation schedule was finalized after some inference, market outcomes precede the November 2026 data freeze, and only twelve dates are represented. Native within-history noncausality, uncertain historical news availability and limited repetitions constrain causal and deployment conclusions. These limitations motivate interpreting the system as a documented decision-support experiment.

The remainder of the paper reviews related work, formalises the forecasting task, describes the agents and experiments, reports results, and discusses limitations and future work.
