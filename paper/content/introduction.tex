\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

At the close of a trading session, a short-horizon equity strategy must
decide whether to enter at the next open or stay in cash. The decision
can draw on recent price and volume, chart patterns and news available
before the cutoff. Even when those signals point upward, the expected
move over a short holding period has to cover trading costs.

Multi-agent systems based on large language models organize such evidence
into specialist reports. TradingAgents~\cite{tradingagents2024} coordinates
analysts across information sources, while FinMem~\cite{yu2025finmem}
organizes financial information in layered memory. For short-horizon
trading, QuantAgent~\cite{xiong2025quantagent} specializes in technical
indicators, chart patterns, trends and risk. When adapting that
architecture to daily Vietnamese equities, an open question is whether
explicit price--volume factors and Vietnamese-language news improve
decisions made from the same technical evidence.

We call the augmented architecture \emph{Our system}. Its Alpha Agent
ranks 85 candidate OHLCV factors and summarizes the selected signals in
the Decision prompt as aggregate direction and vote counts. Its Sentiment
Agent adds a separate summary of eligible Vietnamese news and flags
insufficient coverage. Both reports accompany the same technical and
chart reports used by the local price-only QuantAgent implementation.
This design lets us examine whether the added evidence changes forecasts
and the net return of a three-session LONG-or-CASH strategy.

The main study compares the systems at 102 paired cutoffs across nine
equities and twelve dates in six historical blocks. Smaller repeated
panels examine upstream variability, alpha-only and sentiment-only
variants, and a matched-structure placebo. A separate extension evaluates
five Bayesian in-context learning variants (BICL v1--v5) against controls
that already receive alpha and sentiment. The earlier BICL study and
its follow-up each share one upstream realization per point and use
three Decision repeats. We report their results separately.

In the main study, Our system achieves \FullBA\% balanced accuracy (BA)
versus \BaseBA\% for QuantAgent. Both identify seven of 25 UP outcomes.
Our system makes fewer false LONG forecasts, yielding a \DeltaBA{}
percentage-point BA gain with a descriptive interval of
$[\PrimaryLow,+\PrimaryHigh]$ pp, while its mean net account return is
slightly lower. Logistic-price exceeds Our system on both metrics with
the same 24 LONG decisions. The separate effects of alpha and sentiment
remain uncertain given the limited repetitions, native alpha
preprocessing and uncertain historical news availability. The evaluation
schedule was finalized after some inference, which further limits
generalization from the historical comparison.
